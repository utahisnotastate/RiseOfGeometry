# 02 · Gravity Is Shape

> Gravity isn't a pull. It's the shape of spacetime, and things falling are just going "straight" through a curved space.

**Status:** ✅ Established (general relativity, 1915). 🟡 Open: whether inertia itself comes from the rest of the universe (Mach's principle).

---

## What everyone wishes they'd noticed sooner

Newton's gravity had a mystery at its center that Newton himself hated: how does the Sun "reach out" across empty space and pull the Earth? Einstein's answer was that it doesn't. Mass curves spacetime, and objects in free fall follow the straightest possible paths (**geodesics**) through that curved geometry.

The clue was hiding in plain sight for 300 years. **Everything falls at the same rate**, a feather and a hammer alike (in a vacuum). If gravity were a force like any other, heavier things would respond differently. They don't, because the path belongs to the *space*, not the object.

## Explain it to a kid

Two ants start at the equator of a basketball, side by side, and both walk straight "north." Neither ant turns. Yet they get closer and closer and bump into each other at the top.

Did a force pull them together? No. The **ball is curved**, so two straight paths come together. That's gravity. Earth and the apple both walk "straight" through curved spacetime, and their paths meet.

## Examples you already know

- **Your phone's GPS.** Satellite clocks tick faster than ground clocks by about 38 microseconds per day because they sit higher in Earth's gravity well (the geometry of time is different up there). Without the correction, GPS would drift about 10 km per day.
- **Floating astronauts.** The ISS isn't beyond gravity. It's about 90% as strong up there. Astronauts float because they're in free fall, following a geodesic, so they feel no force.
- **Gravitational lensing.** Light bends around galaxies, producing multiple images and "Einstein rings" of the same distant object.
- **Gravitational waves.** In 2015, LIGO measured a ripple in the *shape of space itself* from two black holes merging 1.3 billion light-years away. It stretched the 4 km detector arms by about 1/1000 the width of a proton.

## The grown-up version

Einstein's field equations tie geometry to matter:

$$
G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}\,T_{\mu\nu}
$$

The left side is pure geometry (curvature of the metric $g_{\mu\nu}$). The right side is energy and momentum. Free particles obey the geodesic equation

$$
\frac{d^2x^\mu}{d\tau^2} + \Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}\frac{dx^\alpha}{d\tau}\frac{dx^\beta}{d\tau}=0 ,
$$

with no force term at all. What Newton called "gravitational force" appears in the Christoffel symbols $\Gamma$, which are properties of the geometry.

### Mach's principle: the open part

Why does a spinning bucket of water climb its walls? Newton said it spins relative to "absolute space." Ernst Mach said it spins relative to **all the distant matter in the universe**. General relativity is partly Machian:

- **Frame dragging** is real. A spinning mass drags spacetime around with it. Gravity Probe B measured this around Earth in 2011, and the LAGEOS satellites confirmed it.
- But GR also allows solutions (like an empty rotating universe) that aren't Machian. Whether inertia *fully* comes from distant matter is still debated.

## Where the original notes went too far

The notes described gravity as masses "shadowing" a cosmic flux that pushes them together. That's **Le Sage's theory of gravity (1748)**, and it was ruled out long ago:

1. A body moving through such a flux would feel drag and slow down. Planetary orbits show no such drag.
2. Absorbing that much flux would heat every object in the universe to incandescence (Maxwell and Poincaré both calculated this).
3. It can't produce the bending of light or the GPS clock shifts, which GR predicts exactly.

The true part is Mach's question about where inertia comes from. That question is still open and still interesting. It doesn't need Le Sage.

## Try it

- **At home:** Drop a heavy book and a sheet of paper, then drop the book with the paper lying *on top* of it. They fall together. Free fall doesn't care about mass.
- **Rubber-sheet demo:** Stretch Lycra over a hoop, put a heavy ball in the middle, and roll marbles. It's a decent (imperfect) picture of curved space. The real version curves *time* too, and that's the part that does most of the work for everyday gravity.
