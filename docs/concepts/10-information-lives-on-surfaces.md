# 10 · Information Lives on Surfaces

> The most information you can fit in a region is set by its **surface area**, not its volume. That one fact is reshaping how physicists think about space itself.

**Status:** ✅ Established for black holes (Bekenstein–Hawking entropy). 🟡 Strongly supported theory (holographic principle, AdS/CFT). ❌ Not supported: the brain as a "receiver" of a universal holographic library.

---

## What everyone wishes they'd noticed sooner

If you asked anyone how much stuff fits in a box, they'd say it depends on the volume. For ordinary stuff, that's true. But in the 1970s, Jacob Bekenstein and Stephen Hawking found that a **black hole's entropy (its information content) is proportional to the area of its surface, not its volume.**

Since you can't pack more information into a region than a black hole of the same size holds (or you'd make one), this sets a **universal limit**: the information inside any region is bounded by its surface area. That's the **holographic principle**. It suggests three-dimensional space might be, in some deep sense, a projection of information stored on a two-dimensional boundary.

## Explain it to a kid

Imagine a huge library where the stories aren't written inside the books. They're written only on the **covers**. If you want to hold more stories, a bigger room doesn't help much. You need more cover, more surface.

The universe seems to work like that at its deepest level. The most you can ever "write" inside any space is limited by the size of its outside skin.

## Examples you already know

- **Holograms** on credit cards: a flat surface stores a 3D-looking image. (The physics principle is named after this, but works differently.)
- **Crumpled paper:** a big sheet fits in your fist because surface area can be folded into a small volume.
- **Your lungs** have about the surface area of half a tennis court (roughly 50–75 m²) folded into your chest, because gas exchange happens on surfaces.
- **Your intestines, tree leaves, coral, radiators, and the gills of fish** all maximize surface for the same reason: the action happens at the boundary.
- **Your brain's wrinkles.** Cortical neurons sit in a sheet about 2–4 mm thick. Bigger brains need proportionally more sheet, and the only way to fit it in a skull is to fold it.

## The grown-up version

**Bekenstein–Hawking entropy:**

$$
S_{BH} = \frac{k_B c^3 A}{4 G \hbar} = \frac{k_B\,A}{4\,\ell_P^2},
$$

one quarter of the horizon area in Planck units. Bekenstein (1981) derived a bound on any system of energy $E$ and radius $R$: $S \le 2\pi k_B R E/(\hbar c)$. Bousso's **covariant entropy bound** (1999) generalized this to arbitrary surfaces in curved spacetime.

**AdS/CFT (Maldacena, 1997)** is a precise, mathematically testable example: a quantum gravity theory in a 5-dimensional anti-de Sitter space is *exactly equivalent* to an ordinary quantum field theory with no gravity on its 4-dimensional boundary. It's one of the most-cited papers in physics history. Whether our actual universe (which isn't AdS) is holographic in the same way is still being worked out.

### Brain folding: the real science

For a smooth ball, area grows like $V^{2/3}$. Across mammals, cortical surface area grows faster than that, so large brains must fold. Mota & Herculano-Houzel (*Science*, 2015) found that folding across species follows a single scaling law: the total cortical area $A_t$, cortical thickness $T$, and exposed area $A_e$ satisfy $A_t\sqrt{T} \propto A_e^{1.25}$, the same law that describes crumpled paper. **The wrinkles are geometry, not a mystery.**

## Where the original notes went too far

- ✅ "Information capacity is bounded by surface area (Bekenstein–Bousso)": **true** in fundamental physics. (Note: that bound is astronomically larger than anything a brain approaches, so it doesn't constrain neuroscience.)
- ✅ "Brains fold to increase surface area": **true.**
- ✅ "Dendrites dominate a neuron's receptive surface": **broadly true.**
- 🟡 "Mental states unfold in ~100 ms microstates from deep to surface structures": EEG "microstates" (typically 60–120 ms) are real and actively studied. Jason Brown's *microgenesis* is a real but minority theory in neuropsychology.
- ❌ "The brain is a receiver tuned to a non-local holographic library; memory isn't stored in the brain": **not supported.** Memories can be located, erased, and even artificially activated in specific neurons (e.g., Tonegawa lab's engram studies, 2012 onward). Brain damage destroys specific memories in predictable ways.
- ❌ "Penfield's stimulation proved total recall": Penfield's patients reported vivid experiences in a small fraction of stimulations, and later analysis suggests many were reconstructions, not recordings.

## Try it

- Cut a sheet of paper into a circle 20 cm across. Crumple it into a ball and measure the ball's diameter. Try with 2 and 3 stacked sheets. You're reproducing the brain-folding scaling law in your kitchen.
- Estimate the surface area of a head of broccoli or a piece of coral by covering it in small stickers.
