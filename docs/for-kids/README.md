# Rise of Geometry: The Kids' Edition 🔺🌻🌀

Grown-ups spent hundreds of years learning that **the shape of things decides how the world works**. Here are the best parts, explained simply. Every idea here is real and has been tested by scientists.

---

### 1. The world saves what's symmetric 🎠
A perfectly round merry-go-round keeps you spinning. A lumpy one slows you down. Round things save spin. Things that are the same everywhere save push. Things that don't change over time save energy.
→ [grown-up version](../concepts/01-symmetry-is-conservation.md)

### 2. Gravity is a bent floor, not a rope 🏀
Two ants walking "straight north" on a basketball bump into each other at the top. No one pulled them; the ball is curved. Earth and the apple meet the same way, because space and time are curved.
→ [grown-up version](../concepts/02-gravity-is-shape.md)

### 3. The arrow that turns by itself ➡️🌍
Walk a triangle on a globe while holding an arrow perfectly straight. When you get back, the arrow points somewhere new! Electricity and magnetism work like that hidden turning.
→ [grown-up version](../concepts/03-forces-are-connections.md)

### 4. Shapes choose the song 🥁
Put sand on a metal plate and make it hum. The sand draws stars and flowers all by itself. A different plate shape makes different pictures. Atoms do this too, which is where the shapes of electron clouds come from.
→ [grown-up version](../concepts/04-shape-picks-the-notes.md)

### 5. The sunflower's secret number 🌻
A sunflower turns 137.5° before placing each new seed. That's the "messiest" turn possible, so seeds never line up, and there are no wasted gaps. That turn comes from the golden ratio. (But no, it isn't hidden in the Mona Lisa!)
→ [grown-up version](../concepts/05-the-golden-ratio-honestly.md)

### 6. Only five perfect dice 🎲
There are only **five** shapes you can build from identical flat faces that meet the same way at every corner. Viruses use one of them (the 20-sided one) to build their shells. And you can't tile a floor with pentagons. Try it!
→ [grown-up version](../concepts/06-the-allowed-shapes.md)

### 7. You can't have half a hole 🍩
A donut has one hole. Squish it, stretch it, still one hole. Some materials carry a hidden "hole count" for electricity, which makes it so exact that scientists use it to define the kilogram.
→ [grown-up version](../concepts/07-topology-knots-and-whole-numbers.md)

### 8. Smoke rings travel far 💨
A spinning donut of air can fly across a room and blow out a candle. Spinning donut shapes hold together, which is why fusion reactors are donut-shaped too.
→ [grown-up version](../concepts/08-vortices-and-tori.md)

### 9. The rewind mirror 🪞
There's a special mirror that sends light back exactly the way it came, undoing blur. Doctors use a sound version to focus on kidney stones. It can't un-break a cup, though. Once energy turns to heat, there's nothing left to rewind.
→ [grown-up version](../concepts/09-waves-that-run-backward.md)

### 10. Stories on the cover 📚
The most information you can fit in a space depends on its **outside skin**, not how big it is inside. That's why your brain is wrinkly: wrinkles squeeze more skin into your head.
→ [grown-up version](../concepts/10-information-lives-on-surfaces.md)

### 11. Empty space jiggles 🌊
Empty space is never truly still. It jiggles a tiny bit. But the best math predicts it should jiggle a trillion-trillion-trillion-trillion-trillion-trillion-trillion-trillion-trillion-trillion times harder than it does. Nobody knows why yet. Maybe you'll figure it out!
→ [grown-up version](../concepts/11-the-vacuum.md)

### 12. One circle makes all of logic ⭕
Draw a circle, and you have an inside and an outside: yes and no. With two tiny rules about circles, you can build all the logic inside a computer. And computers only *have* to make heat when they throw information away.
→ [grown-up version](../concepts/12-boundaries-logic-and-computing.md)

### 13. Is everything just shapes? 🤔
Scientists keep finding that the rules of the universe are really rules about shapes. But to know *which* shapes are real, you still have to go out and look. Thinking alone can't tell you.
→ [grown-up version](../concepts/13-can-geometry-replace-physics.md)

## A scientist's superpower 🦸

The best thing about science is that **you're allowed to check**. If someone tells you a shape has magic powers, ask: *"How could we test that? What would happen if it's wrong?"* Real discoveries survive those questions. That's how you know they're real.
